How it works

From one photo to a report you can act on

1

Photo

You add one leaf photo, the plant name and your state. The photo is analysed on this computer and is never stored.

2

Symptoms & disease

A ResNet-50 with two heads reads the photo: one scores 25 visible symptoms, the other the diseases of that crop. Grad-CAM shows where it looked.

3

Weather

The last 7 days and next 3 days of weather for your place (Open-Meteo) are compared with the conditions each disease needs.

4

Report

If one disease scores above the threshold on a fully covered crop, you get one named disease. Otherwise you get the 3 closest matches, with tests to tell them apart and state-wise advice.

So far, on validation, 60% names about 6 more field photos in 100 for about 2 points less accuracy, and slightly more false names on non-crop photos. Field ranges are 95% intervals (small set). Lab photos are named only 74.5% of the time because 5 crops with a single trained class (blueberry, citrus, raspberry, soybean, squash) are never named by design; on the other lab photos it is 99.7%. Not-named photos still get the 3 closest matches. The score is a heuristic, not a probability of being correct.

Good to know

  • Field photos are harder than lab photos. The PlantDoc test is small (3–12 photos per class).
  • Diseases without training photos are ranked by symptom match only and are never named on their own; they appear among the closest matches.
  • Chemical advice lists active ingredients only, never doses. Follow your State Agricultural University schedule and your KVK.
  • Only the place name and coordinates go to Open-Meteo for weather.
